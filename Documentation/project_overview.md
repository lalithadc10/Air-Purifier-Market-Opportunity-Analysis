#  PROVE Framework

I used the **PROVE framework** to structure the analysis and keep the project focused on business decision-making rather than only dashboard creation.

## P → Problem

### What business problem are we solving?

An air-purifier company needs to understand:

> **Where should we focus our market, what pollution problems should our products address, and which customer markets should be investigated further?**

The analysis is designed to support decisions around:

- Market prioritization
- Product requirements
- Pollution-related product positioning
- Potential city/state expansion

---

## R → Research

Before analyzing the data, I considered the business context and identified the key signals that could influence the opportunity.

The project combines multiple datasets covering:

- Air Quality
- Disease outcomes
- Population
- Vehicle registrations

### Key business questions included:

- Where is AQI highest?
- Which pollutants are dominant?
- Which locations are pollution hotspots?
- How does AQI vary over time?
- Are there differences between weekdays and weekends?
- How do health outcomes vary across states?
- How large are the potential markets?
- What additional market signals can support prioritization?

---

## O → Observe

The analysis was performed using Power BI, Power Query, and DAX.

### Key observations:

- PM10 emerged as the dominant pollutant in the dataset.
- PM10 and PM2.5 had high numbers of observations.
- AQI severity varies considerably across locations.
- Several locations showed very high AQI levels during the selected analysis period.
- Disease burden varies significantly across states.
- Population size provides important context when interpreting AQI.
- Vehicle registration data provides an additional signal for understanding state-level market size.
- AQI patterns can vary across cities and time periods.

The objective was to separate **observations from business insights** rather than simply reporting numbers.

---

## V → Value

The analysis can help an air-purifier business think about market opportunity from multiple dimensions.

### Potential business actions:

- Identify high-pollution locations for deeper market research.
- Consider PM10 and PM2.5 when evaluating filtration requirements.
- Prioritize markets where pollution exposure and population are both significant.
- Use health and population indicators as supporting signals.
- Consider vehicle and mobility data as an additional market-size indicator.
- Avoid making market decisions using AQI alone.

The dashboard is intended to provide **evidence for further investigation**, rather than automatically selecting a market.

---

## E → Execute

The next step would be to validate the analytical findings with actual market data.

Potential next steps include:

1. Shortlist high-potential cities or states.
2. Conduct customer research in those markets.
3. Analyze existing air-purifier competitors.
4. Compare product features and pricing.
5. Test product demand through pilot markets.
6. Measure customer response and sales.
7. Compare actual market performance against the dashboard's opportunity signals.
8. Scale only after validating the initial results.

#  Key Business Insights

### 1. PM10 is a major pollutant signal

PM10 emerged as the dominant pollutant in the dataset.

**Business implication:**  
PM10 should be considered when evaluating air-purifier filtration and product requirements.

---

### 2. Pollution is geographically different

AQI varies considerably across locations.

**Business implication:**  
A single nationwide strategy may not be appropriate. Market research should consider location-specific pollution conditions.

---

### 3. High AQI does not automatically mean high market opportunity

A location can have severe pollution but still require additional market validation.

**Business implication:**  

Market prioritization should consider:

**AQI + Population + Health Burden + Market Size**

---

### 4. Health data adds another layer of context

Disease data shows differences in health burden across states.

**Business implication:**  
Health indicators can be used as a supporting signal when studying the potential impact of poor air quality.

> The analysis shows relationships in the data and should not be interpreted as proof of direct causation.

---

### 5. Market size matters

Population and vehicle-registration data provide additional context for understanding the size and characteristics of potential markets.

**Business implication:**  
The business should evaluate both **need and market potential** before selecting locations for expansion.
